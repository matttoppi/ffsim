# Draft-intelligence giant accuracy audit

Batch: `20260815T043426-52dba2d4`  
Engine: `DECISION_ENGINE_VERSION = 7`  
Audit date: 2026-08-15  
Scope: 150 completed synthetic drafts; audit only; no engine, policy, scorer, frontend, or test changes

## Executive verdict

Version 7 is not yet reliable as a human-facing draft advisor for these league configurations. The batch completed cleanly: telemetry invariants and paired-Q identities passed. That does not extend to the racing/final-gate contract, which failed targeted replay. The central problem is external accuracy: following the advisor produces timing and roster shapes that experienced drafters would repeatedly reject.

The strongest failures, ranked by user-facing severity, are:

1. **Critical — K/DEF are recommended far too early.** Of the 300 K/DEF selections, 223 (74.3%) came before the final three rounds, affecting 146/150 drafts. Ninety-eight came before the user had completed `QB/RB2/WR2/TE`; 70 of those were called clear leaders. The comparable near-format human rate was 3/128 rosters before core, and current public strategy guidance still treats kicker as a final-round pick and defense as a final-round or final-couple-round position. The artifact family was already acknowledged in ADR-032; its batch-wide severity was not.

2. **Critical — the advisor delays basic starters while accumulating RB/TE/QB depth.** One hundred drafts (66.7%) selected TE2 before QB1, versus 0/128 near-format human rosters. There were 117 second-QB/TE headlines while at least one dedicated `QB/RB2/WR2/TE` slot remained, affecting 114 drafts. Median core completion was round 11 in ten-team and round 10 in twelve-team drafts, versus 7.5 and 8 in exact human comparators. This new batch result is plausibly enabled by the terminal objective/projections, end-only feasibility policy, and soft synthetic opponent room together; the batch does not isolate one cause.

3. **High — the synthetic opponents are legal but materially nonhuman, so the impressive calibration has no external human-validity implication.** Among 23,946 opponent picks, 1,004 were at least five rounds ahead of ADP and 27 K/DEF picks occurred in rounds 2–5. Indianapolis DEF went at pick 17 with ADP 225 and logged probability `3.41e-6`. The same opponent model generates the rooms and the survival forecasts; a Brier score of 0.102 is valid same-generator calibration, not human availability accuracy.

4. **High — the advisor makes high-survival reaches with weak conventional candidate-local scarcity evidence.** A conservative descriptive screen found 112 recommendations in 99 drafts at least two rounds ahead of ADP where ADP was beyond the next turn, predicted survival was at least 70%, the recorded candidate-local same-position drop was below 8.5, and the state was not must-fill. Eighty-two were kickers and 25 were tight ends; 96 were clear calls, and 70 had nonpositive VONA. At draft `:0150`, pick 71, Oronde Gadsden was taken three rounds ahead of ADP despite 100% predicted survival and `−10.01` VONA, then Harold Fannin was taken three picks later. The screen does not rule out portfolio scarcity already embedded in paired Q.

5. **High — racing can stop before the final 0.1 gate is resolved, contrary to ADR-032.** At draft `:0123`, pick 30, the 150-rollout board headlined Rashee Rice as a toss-up despite a `−2.070` paired mean versus Breece Hall. A flat replay made Breece clear at 225 (`+2.448`, lower `+0.789`) and 300 (`+2.333`, lower `+0.870`). At draft `:0080`, pick 40, a recorded `+1.852`, lower `+0.098` toss-up became a clear `+3.005`, lower `+1.587` at 225. Racing tests whether a rival can plausibly beat the leader by zero; the final gate admits rivals within 0.1. Those are not equivalent bounds.

6. **Medium-high — the toss-up headline and Why copy overstate what the evidence says.** Toss-ups were 1,344/2,384 calls. The median tied tier contained 34 players; 498 toss-up headlines had their entire paired interval below the runner, and 298 had a point estimate at least 0.1 worse. Raw K/DEF VONA is deliberately ignored by tie ordering as an artifact but is still displayed as “Waiting costs points.” A replay also disproved the claim that a surviving player is necessarily redrafted on the next turn.

7. **Medium — roster balance and bye construction are visibly off even though every roster is legal.** Ten-team users finished with seven or more RBs in 16/67 drafts versus 0/80 near-human rosters; twelve-team users averaged 4.87 WR versus 5.54 exact-human. Fifteen of 122 QB2 builds paired same-bye quarterbacks, and a PPR-adjusted cached-projection proxy placed at least four likely starters on one bye in 28/150 rosters.

8. **Medium — the >20-second latency tail remains a release blocker.** Ready latency was median 9.17 seconds, P95 20.11, maximum 26.59; 124/2,384 calls (5.20%) exceeded 20 seconds. Every one was in rounds 1–5. The prior v7 batch had virtually the same >20-second rate, so the tail persists.

There are real positives. All 150 user rosters were legal, every cap was respected, there were no third-QB/TE recommendations, all user picks followed the headline, telemetry identities were exact, and the user optimizer dominated its own synthetic environment. Those successes do not offset the external draft-advice failures above.

### League configurations

The scenario rotation contains two one-QB full-PPR snake formats:

- Ten teams, 17 rounds: `QB/2RB/2WR/TE/2FLEX/K/DEF/7BN`, four-point passing TD, and `+0.5` points per TE reception.
- Twelve teams, 15 rounds: `QB/2RB/2WR/TE/FLEX/K/DEF/6BN`, four-point passing TD, no TE premium.

There were 67 ten-team and 83 twelve-team drafts. Every user slot appeared six or seven times.

### Version-7 decision contract

The audit judges the implemented ADR-031/032 contract, not an older static ranking. Root candidates are compared on mean deterministic projected value of the completed user roster across coupled rest-of-draft continuations. The scorer credits legal weekly starter value over replacement plus the configured discounted bench-asset term. The future-user policy uses scorer-aligned marginal values with end-of-draft feasibility. Take-now urgency is supposed to appear inside Q through those continuations, not as an additive ranking bonus. A candidate is “clear” only when the Q leader’s paired lower bound clears the 0.1-point practical gate; practical ties are ordered by VONA then market ADP, with K/DEF urgency zeroed only in that tie ordering.

### Human comparator

The local history database supplies three exact-format drafts:

- Ten-team: draft `1257451412544028673`, 10 rosters from the same 2025 `+0.5` TEP league and exact 17-round format.
- Twelve-team: drafts `1130247723615281152` and `1257089821877219329`, 24 rosters from 2024–25 with the exact 15-round format.

A wider near-format cohort keeps the same non-bench starters and 14–17 rounds while permitting bench-count differences: eight ten-team drafts/80 rosters and four twelve-team drafts/48 rosters. These cohorts include, rather than add to, the exact drafts. “Near” matches the normalized PPR label, team count, rounds, and roster slots; detailed scoring such as TEP and K/DEF settings is not uniformly matched. The observations are correlated within drafts and the draft-level sample is small, so they are used for positional timing and roster shape, not player-level 2026 judgments. Current public guidance is a corroborating, not primary, standard: [FantasyPros recommends kicker in the final round](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/06/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-how-to-draft-kickers-2026/), [defense in the final couple of rounds](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/06/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-how-to-draft-defenses-2026/), and [late-round RB/WR upside over early special teams](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/07/2026-fantasy-football-late-round-draft-strategy/).

The current market snapshot is `6f9bbe71475aa0daafc44d2d3f7c5f3760ee22af5c37cafd63dc88b21c84ba43`, FantasyPros/Sleeper PPR 1QB, 334 rows. It has no team-count, dispersion, or tier metadata and does not encode the ten-team TE premium. Player-level reach conclusions therefore require the advisor’s own survival and cliff evidence to say waiting was safe.

### Review protocol

The audit used all 150 drafts for rates, calibration, latency, final-roster shape, and opponent behavior. It also hand-read 192 decisions in 12 drafts. For each configuration, slots were divided into early/middle/late bands; one median-flag and one high-flag draft was selected per band. That sample is risk-stratified and supports mechanism diagnosis, not prevalence estimates.

“Systematic” means at least five independent sessions and at least 5% of eligible drafts/states, or at least ten recurring cases, spanning at least three slots. A single legality/cap defect would still count as a defect. “Recurring” is used for a real multi-session problem below that threshold; “one-off” is not generalized.

Counterfactual caution matters. A realized opponent path reveals whether a passed player survived, not the full roster value of an unobserved alternate draft. This report calls the directly observable quantity “realized availability” or “next-turn marginal shortfall.” It reserves “paired regret” for the recorded coupled rollout delta.

Evidence rows always name the full session UUID. After the first full draft reference, `:NNNN` is shorthand for `synthetic:20260815T043426-52dba2d4:NNNN`.

### Handcuffs and low-upside depth

- Session `b6a31c74-8b47-4314-950b-432c696cc81f`, draft `:0090`, pick 115/r12: Kyle Monangai, Chicago RB2, after the user already rostered D’Andre Swift. Roster-before was `RB6/WR1/TE2/QB1/DEF1`. ## 5. Urgency, scarcity, and high-survival reaches

### High-survival/low-local-cliff reach screen

A recommendation entered the conservative reach review set only when all of these held:

- ADP was at least two league rounds after the current pick and at or after the next user turn;
- recorded survival was at least 70%;
- recorded same-position drop was below 8.5 season points;
- the roster was not in a must-fill tail.

This produced 112 states in 99/150 drafts: 82 K, 25 TE, and 5 DEF; no RB or WR. Ninety-six were clear leaders and 70 had nonpositive VONA. Median reach was 2.90 rounds in ten-team and 2.33 in twelve-team drafts. The repeated names were Harrison Mevis 79, Oronde Gadsden 24, Green Bay DEF 5, Chris Boswell 3, and Hunter Henry 1. The screen finds no strong candidate-local scarcity signal under these audit thresholds; candidate-taken positional drop is not the paired wait branch and does not exclude multi-player portfolio scarcity embedded in Q.

Session `8e68b036-02a8-4758-88c2-b6df22258f6a`, draft `:0150`, pick 71/r6, is a direct trust failure:

- The twelve-team roster was `RB3/WR2`, with no QB or TE.
- Oronde Gadsden, ADP 107, was taken at pick 71: a three-round reach.
- He was clear over Jadarian Price by `+0.1941`, interval `[+0.1859,+0.2024]`, despite survival `1.000`, VONA `−10.011`, same-position drop `−10.011`, and current marginal value `0.000`.
- At the next user pick, 74, the system selected Harold Fannin, ADP 67, clear by `+9.793`.
- An actual-seed wait replay took Price at 71 and Fannin at 74. Gadsden was still available at 74 but the future policy did **not** draft him; an opponent took him at 106.

The sequence is a human-facing sequencing/reach failure and directly contradicts the Why claim that a surviving player is automatically recovered. It does **not** prove that the `+0.194` paired-Q edge is false: taking Gadsden can preserve the option to roster both him and Fannin, while waiting forces a choice at 74. An exact paired-branch objective replay would be required to label the edge itself an artifact.

### What “realized regret” can and cannot mean here

The recorded paired Q edge is the valid take-versus-best-alternative expectation. Raw VONA and positional drop are not interchangeable with that edge: they are computed from continuations conditioned on taking the candidate, while exact-player survival is read from the best-alternative branch. Combining those fields as if they were one counterfactual produces false precision.

The actual draft supplies one draw. It can establish that a passed player was gone, and which same-position values were available next; it cannot establish the alternate final roster without replay. This audit therefore reports availability misses separately from paired Q. The Gadsden replay is stronger evidence about policy sequencing and the explanation contract because it reconstructs the recorded seed and observes the future policy itself; it still does not reconstruct both terminal-objective branches.

Accordingly, batch-wide realized take-versus-wait **final-roster regret is not identifiable from this telemetry**. The 35-state 4.11-point mean is a next-board marginal diagnostic, while recorded paired Q is modeled expected regret. A true realized counterfactual rate would require exact paired-branch replays or persisted alternate terminal rosters for every state; only the single actual-seed policy trace above was replayed here.

## 6. The 0.1 gate, toss-ups, racing, and continuity

### Gate population

There were 1,040 clear calls (43.6%) and 1,344 toss-ups (56.4%). The late board collapses into enormous practical-tie sets: mean toss-up size was 27.2, median 34, P90 42, maximum 44; 984/1,344 toss-ups (73.2%) had at least 20 co-leaders, 883 (65.7%) at least 30, and 835 (62.1%) tied every refined candidate. In 1,037 toss-ups (77.2%), the headline’s serialized current marginal was exactly zero.

Headline direction inside those ties is often contrary to Q:

- 737/1,344 toss-up headlines had a negative paired mean versus the runner.
- 498/1,344 had the entire paired 95% interval below zero.
- 298/1,344 had a mean at least 0.1 worse.
- 22 had an absolute paired mean over 1.0 point.

That is allowed by the practical gate: it asks whether the Q leader’s lower-bound advantage exceeds 0.1, then lets VONA/market choose any admitted headline. It is not accurately described as “the same final-roster value within simulation precision” when equality is outside the interval.

Example: session `a25f1ffb-08e9-4d61-bc3e-34918744e160`, draft `:0040`, pick 74 selected Jalen Hurts as QB2 while a skill flex was open. His interval versus Oronde Gadsden was entirely negative, `[−0.4535,−0.0136]`, yet the UI called the options a statistical tie.

### Racing/gate mismatch — new defect

ADR-032 states that the racing stop and final practical gate use the same quantity and threshold, so they “can never disagree about decisiveness.” The code uses different comparisons:

- Racing retains a rival only if `CI_upper(rival − leader) >= 0` and stops when no retained rival’s plausible advantage exceeds 0.1.
- The final gate admits a rival when `CI_lower(leader − rival) <= 0.1`, equivalently `CI_upper(rival − leader) >= −0.1`.

A rival in `[-0.1, 0)` is dropped by racing but is a practical co-leader. More importantly, racing can stop once no rival can plausibly win even though additional samples would move the leader’s lower bound beyond 0.1 and turn a toss-up into a clear call.

Two flat-budget replays confirm material consequences:

1. Session `b0537364-c590-4bb5-be73-a68a707e41f0`, draft `:0123`, pick 30/r3:
   - Recorded/stage 150 Q leader: Breece Hall. Practical co-leaders: Breece, Rashee Rice, Colston Loveland. Racing survivors: Breece only; `max_advantage=0`, so production stopped.
   - The VONA tiebreak headlined Rashee Rice, paired `−2.070` versus Breece, interval `[−4.097,−0.043]`.
   - Flat 225: Breece clear over Rice by `+2.448`, lower `+0.789`.
   - Flat 300: Breece clear by `+2.333`, lower `+0.870`.
   - At adjacent pick 31 the advisor then headlined Colston Loveland over the still-available Breece with the same recorded `−2.070` edge. A user following both turn picks skips the same recorded Q leader twice; the flat p30 replay shows that the first skip disagreed with the engine's configured full-budget estimate. It does not by itself establish human-ground-truth value.

2. Session `59719bcd-8a46-4db1-92ab-82a15b158fb6`, draft `:0080`, pick 40/r4:
   - Recorded/stage 150: Cam Skattebo over Zay Flowers by `+1.852`, lower `+0.098`; labeled toss-up. Racing retained only Cam and stopped.
   - Flat 225: Cam clear `+3.005`, lower `+1.587`.
   - Flat 300: Cam clear `+2.526`, lower `+1.336`.

A third replay shows that elimination can make the final co-leader set incomplete even when the 300-budget result remains a practical tie. At `99dfa900-2d08-4fd5-82e1-c67b2d7456c2`, draft `:0112`, pick 35, Josh Jacobs was practical at 225 (leader edge `+1.084`, lower `+0.024`) and remained practical in flat 300 (`+0.930`, lower `+0.000`). Racing had eliminated him, so he was absent from the production final co-leaders. The Loveland headline happened not to change because its VONA was 7.22 versus Jacobs’ `−4.9`; the board contract still disagreed with the gate.

The stored payload contains direct batch-wide disagreement evidence: in 7/2,384 ready states (0.29%), an eliminated `screened_candidate` was still marked `is_top_tier` at its deepest recorded stage—six at 150 rollouts and one at 225. For example, `fa17a8cc-c8ef-45f0-8c96-80859fa00357`, draft `:0115`, pick 31 had Cam Skattebo practical at stage 150 (leader edge `+1.599`, lower `+0.097`) when the zero-bound survivor rule dropped him; a separate flat-225 replay later put him outside the gate (`lower +0.342`). At `6d5105b0-8269-4731-ab76-e805413c87b0`, draft `:0081`, pick 22, Rashee Rice was likewise practical when dropped after stage 150 (`+1.732`, lower `+0.021`); flat 300 later confirmed him outside (`lower +0.124`). Neither changed its final headline, but both expose the bound mismatch in recorded states.

Across the batch, 1,194 toss-ups stopped at 150; 15 had an absolute paired mean above 0.5, 11 above one point, and 11 had CI width above two points. Seven of the 15 headlined a candidate at least 0.5 worse by point estimate. The replays prove the defect exists, not its full flip rate. Intermediate leaders and stop reasons were not recorded, so a batch-wide counterfactual rate would require replaying every state.

### Clear-call quality

Every clear call satisfied the encoded gate; no identity violation was found. However, 250/1,040 clear calls (24.0%) beat the runner in fewer than half of continuations. The mean is being driven by rarer, larger positive branches. That is valid for a risk-neutral expected-value objective, but “clear leader” needs risk framing.

Kicker is the extreme: 119/130 clear kicker calls won fewer than half of paired continuations; mean win share was 24.1%.

One hundred seventy-two clear calls had lower bounds in `[0.1,0.2)`; 129/172 were K/DEF. Version 7 is therefore converting many barely-practical special-team edges into authoritative calls.

- Session `4a6d0aab-5196-4679-a588-0b27d3e2dca6`, draft `:0056`, pick 110/r10: Harrison Mevis over Bo Nix, `+0.2746`, interval `[+0.1676,+0.3815]`, but ahead in only 14.7% of continuations. Survival was 85.3%, VONA/current marginal `+7.591`; roster-before was `RB5/WR2/TE1/DEF1`, no QB.
- Exact marginal replay reproduced Mevis at `+7.591` and every evaluated QB at zero under the scorer. This is not a serialization error: deterministic K points above the K replacement cutline are valued while the remaining QBs sit at their replacement floor.

### Adjacent-pick continuity

There were 195 back-to-back user-pick transitions at snake turns. The previous runner remained in the next evaluated board in 184; it became the next headline in 85 (46.2%). In 89 transitions, the next headline’s position differed from the prior runner’s position.

Across all nonterminal next-user-turn transitions, 1,320 prior runners actually survived. Of those, 1,268 (96.1%) remained somewhere on the next board, 1,227 (93.0%) were refined, 1,151 (87.2%) remained top-tier, and 476 (36.1%) became the next headline. Of the 52 absent runners, 46 shared the selected headline’s position and had become cap-infeasible; the other six were round 14–16 tail states. Sticky admission is working. The trust failure is ranking/racing, not wholesale candidate disappearance.

Roster marginals legitimately change after the first turn pick, so those rates alone are not defects. The `:0123` p30/p31 trace above is a demonstrated trust-breaking discontinuity because the same Breece comparison and same racing stop persist across both picks. The Gadsden/Fannin p71/p74 sequence is another: the advisor reaches for a 100%-survival TE, then chooses a different TE on its next turn.

## 7. Value-model artifacts leaking into advice

### K/DEF: documented artifact, newly severe manifestation

ADR-032 explicitly notes that positive streamable K/DEF VONA can be a below-replacement feasible-pool artifact. Live tie ordering zeroes K/DEF urgency for that reason. The raw artifact does not feed paired Q, but it still reaches users directly in the payload and Why panel. Early clear K/DEF is a distinct scorer/rollout-Q mechanism: a clear edge selects the position before the tie tiebreak has any chance to neutralize urgency.

Of the 300 K/DEF headlines, 208 had raw VONA above one point: 84 K clear, 72 DEF clear, and 52 DEF toss-up. For 52/53 DEF toss-ups, the UI says the tied order is decided by waiting cost even though DEF urgency was explicitly ignored and market ADP selected the headline.

The exact Mevis replay shows that distinct mechanism beyond raw-VONA serialization: the scorer genuinely assigns a stable season-long K edge over replacement. Humans generally discount kicker projection stability and retain streaming flexibility; this objective does not. The known VONA artifact is real in explanations, while “zero K/DEF urgency in ties” is separately insufficient to keep clear K/DEF Q edges out of advice.

### Bench assets and bye coverage

ADR-031 documents that the future policy’s seat summary approximates full weekly bye coverage and can overprice bench assets by a few points. A root-state exact marginal comparison across 96,366 candidate-stage rows quantified the residual in that policy input; it is not a paired-Q or continuation replay:

This does not establish a phantom Q edge because the take path retains a different two-TE option set. It does establish that survival, conditional harvesting, and paired terminal value are distinct quantities that the current payload and prose conflate.

### Market-context mismatch

The scorer understands the ten-team `+0.5` TEP configuration, but the shared opponent/ADP market is generic PPR 1QB: `team_count=NULL`, no TEP adjustment, and no tier/dispersion fields. The same board is used in both formats. That lets the engine value TEs under TEP while opponents select them under a generic market, creating a risk that their modeled survival is too generous for the very league where the advisor is most TE-aggressive. It also weakens player-level scarcity evidence in both ten- and twelve-team rooms.

This mismatch is new and external to ADR-031’s scorer alignment. The ten-team user took first TE at median r4 while opponents waited to r8 and exact same-league humans to r6.5.

## 9. Evidence and explanation quality

### What is correct

Formal payload consistency was excellent in all 2,384 ready states:

- Every headline was present exactly once.
- Co-leader membership agreed with `decision_status`.
- Headline/runner IDs matched the paired delta.
- `Q(headline) − Q(runner)` equaled the paired delta.
- Every clear lower bound exceeded 0.1.
- Later-alternative probability mass was normalized.

The flaws are semantic and user-facing, not malformed telemetry.

### “Clear” does not mean usually better

The panel reports a mean and the percentage of continuations won, but labels the result “Best final roster” and the recommendation a clear leader. The Mevis p110 example says `+0.275`, clear, while also saying he comes out ahead in only 15% of continuations. That is expected-value consistency driven by rare larger wins, not the ordinary-language meaning of a clear pick. The copy should say “best expected value” and expose the tail asymmetry.

### VONA is presented as paired wait regret when it is not

Headline opportunity evidence is measured on continuations conditioned on **taking that headline**. The exact-player survival value is measured on the best-alternative branch. Yet the UI says “if you pass” and “Passing now gives back about X points,” treating candidate-local VONA/later alternatives as the paired pass branch. This branch mixing affects 2,222 headlines with later-alternative payloads and is especially visible when Mevis has paired edge `+0.275` but displayed waiting cost `+7.591`.

K/DEF makes the contradiction explicit: 208 headlines display artifact VONA above one even though tie ordering deliberately zeros it.

### The harvest claim is false in a recorded replay

For 589 clear calls, the UI says that when the player survives, “passing still drafts him there.” Telemetry records availability, not conditional next-user selection. The `:0150` p71 replay supplies a direct counterexample: Gadsden survived with predicted probability 100%, but the future policy selected Fannin at the next turn and left Gadsden for an opponent at 106.

### “Starting-lineup value” omits the bench term

The UI calls the Q delta “season points of starting-lineup value.” The final roster objective also credits non-starting assets at the ADR-026/031 bench factor. A Q edge can therefore include bench value. That matters precisely in the second-QB/TE-before-core cases this audit flags.

### “Strongest asset to hold or trade” has no trade model

All 1,344 toss-ups receive copy saying the market-tiebreak headline is the strongest asset to hold or trade. There is no trade simulation; generic ADP is only a proxy. In the giant late tiers, the statement converts a deterministic tie ordering into an unsupported product promise.

Synthetic event duration has not been validated as identical to warm-cache browser-perceived latency—it may include CLI preparation not present in a live request—so the 2-second comparison is a release risk requiring matched live measurement, not a definitive UI SLO failure. The ready metric is directly the audit-loop measure, and its >20-second tail fails that loop's stated blocker threshold.

All 124 >20-second ready calls were in rounds 1–5: 124/750 early states (16.5%). Middle-round maximum was 17.9 seconds and late maximum 8.80. Screen-to-ready refinement added median 1.381 seconds, P95 4.403, maximum 8.184, so the broad early screen is the main cost.

Worst state: `1db137a1-fc10-4cd5-8cc7-4e3e35798e62`, draft `:0135`, pick 12/r1, Justin Jefferson, two-way toss-up: screen 24.175 seconds, ready 26.592.

The prior v7 batch `20260814T205948-67f958bc` had 4/77 >20 seconds (5.19%), P95 19.913, maximum 24.498. The tail rate is effectively unchanged.

### Refinement depth

- 150 rollouts: 2,205/2,384 (92.5%).
- 225: 43 (1.8%).
- 300: 136 (5.7%).
- Screen-to-ready headline changes: 144 (6.04%); round-11-and-later states changed 73/884 (8.26%).
- Twenty-four clear calls stopped at 150 with lower bound ≤0.110; 19 were K/DEF.

The two flat replays in section 6 prove early-stop instability relative to the configured 225/300-rollout evaluator, and one changes the human-facing headline. Telemetry did not retain intermediate racing leaders, co-leaders, or stop reasons, so the full false-stop rate cannot be recovered from this batch alone.

## 11. Other findings

### Within-generator outcomes are strong but not validating

The user optimizer had mean title probability 17.65%, median 17.33%, and ranked first by title in 102/150 drafts (68%). Ten-team mean was 18.76% versus neutral 10%; twelve-team 16.76% versus neutral 8.33%. Mean playoff probability was 75.90% and expected wins 8.206.

These outcomes are correlated secondary telemetry, not literal self-play. The advisor optimizes deterministic projected roster value; opponents use the Sleeper-ADP sampler; title/playoff/win results come from a separate 300-world championship simulation. TE2-before-QB1 drafts were not punished in that simulation: they averaged 17.92% title and ranked first 69% of the time, versus 17.12%/66% otherwise. The result shows strong performance inside this shared synthetic generator, not human advice accuracy or direct optimization of title equity.

There is also a material slot gradient worth future validation: twelve-team slot 1 averaged 23.67% title and ranked first 6/6, while slot 12 averaged 11.48% and ranked first 1/7. Some early-slot advantage is plausible, but this magnitude could interact with opponent softness and the deterministic value objective.

### Telemetry read/write concurrency

The discarded batch demonstrates that a long read against the default rollback-journal database can block the writer long enough to orphan a live session. This did not contaminate the authoritative batch. It is an operational auditability defect and should be addressed before routine overnight audits.

## 13. Prioritized candidate fixes — described, not implemented

1. **Revalidate the human-facing objective and roster timing before more tuning.** Add held-out, draft-level checks for K/DEF-before-core, TE2-before-QB1, first-QB round, core-fill round, WR/RB final depth, and same-bye backup pairs. Determine which deviations are projection/scorer choices versus policy approximations before choosing a mechanism.

2. **Keep streamable-position artifacts out of both Q calls and explanations.** Investigate why deterministic K/DEF cutline value produces clear r8–r12 edges; separately prevent known raw VONA artifacts from being presented as pass regret. Tie-order zeroing alone is not sufficient.

3. **Make racing use the same practical bound as the final gate, then validate flat-budget equivalence at scale.** The current zero-bound survivor test is not the 0.1 practical gate. Record and compare the flat-300 headline/status, especially for high-width 150-stop toss-ups.

4. **Human-calibrate opponents and availability out of sample.** Revisit the 15% full-support reach component, onesie timing, and RB/WR bench shapes using leakage-safe real drafts. Condition the market on team count and TE premium or explicitly model the missing context. Do not treat synthetic self-calibration as product calibration.

5. **Audit the scorer-aligned future policy’s dynamic path, not only its terminal ordinal agreement.** Quantify exact-player harvest conditional on survival, same-turn/next-turn substitution, high-survival reaches, and the cost of end-only feasibility. Replay both paired terminal-objective branches before classifying an edge as phantom. The Gadsden trace shows that “survives” and “is harvested” are different events.

6. **Correct the Why-panel evidence contract.** Label mean-Q calls “best expected value”; show rare-tail/majority behavior; separate paired Q from approximate VONA; use the correct branch for later alternatives; remove unsupported trade claims; and do not assert conditional redrafting without recording it.

7. **Profile and reduce the early broad-screen tail without weakening rollout correctness.** All >20-second calls are in rounds 1–5 and screen time dominates. Preserve coupled sampling and statistical budgets; target measured preparation/evaluation work.

8. **Record richer racing and wait telemetry.** Persist per-stage leader, candidate IDs, paired intervals, practical co-leaders, survivor set, stop reason, and conditional future-user picks. Without this, audit must replay expensive states and cannot calculate a full false-stop or non-harvest rate.

9. **Harden telemetry concurrency.** Use a writer-safe audit strategy—such as WAL with explicit busy handling or immutable post-batch snapshots—so read-only audits cannot block a live batch commit.

No candidate fix was implemented in this audit.

## Appendix B — evidence and interpretation limits

- `candidates` are authoritative final-depth racing survivors. `screened_candidates` are a mixed-depth watchlist and cannot be ranked as peers.
- Survival is conditional on each candidate’s `best_wait_candidate_id`, not a universal state forecast.
- `later_alternatives`, VONA, and positional drop are candidate-taken-branch opportunity evidence; survival is best-alternative-branch evidence.
- One realized draft path measures availability, not counterfactual final-roster value.
- Completed equity uses the observed final rosters and 300 season worlds and remains secondary telemetry.
- Same-model survival/outcome calibration does not establish human calibration.
- Human history is small and clustered by draft; exact/near comparisons are positional anchors, not 2026 player rankings.
- The market snapshot lacks team-count, TEP, tier, and dispersion context.
